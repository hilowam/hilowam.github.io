The central idea

A longer view.
A closer look.

Dense, short-horizon video prediction captures robot–object interactions, but provides limited context for how a long task has progressed and where it is heading. Simply extending a single visual trajectory mixes these temporal roles and adds prediction uncertainty and computation.

HiLo-WAM explicitly separates long-horizon task evolution from local visual dynamics. A sparse visual stream captures historical task context and coarse future evolution; a dense stream models the immediate robot–object interaction. Both share a Video DiT and guide an Action DiT through structured cross-stream attention. Progressive stream-wise inference caches visual representations while actions continue to refine.

TL;DR

Two temporal roles.

Core question

How can a World Action Model understand long-horizon task evolution without losing fine-grained control?

HiLo-WAM decouples the visual world into a sparse long-horizon stream for historical context and coarse future evolution, and a dense local visual dynamics stream for precise robot–object interactions. Structured cross-stream attention lets both streams jointly guide action generation.

Problem

One trajectory mixes two dynamics.

Existing WAMs mainly predict dense videos over short windows. Extending that same process to long horizons increases uncertainty and computation while entangling task-level progression with local interaction dynamics.

Design

Model each temporal role at its scale.

The long-horizon stream samples history and future with stride k; the local stream keeps dense unit-stride observations. A shared Video DiT and an Action DiT learn their dependencies together.

Finding

Context and contact are complementary.

Long-horizon context keeps the robot on the right task trajectory, while local dynamics resolves the current grasp, alignment, and contact. Progressive inference reduces the extra visual-modeling cost.

01 / Method

A WAM that separates
the temporal roles.

HiLo-WAM follows a dual-DiT architecture: a shared Video DiT models two visual streams, while an Action DiT generates the robot action chunk from their coordinated context.

Long-horizon stream

Where is the task heading?

Sparse historical frames track task context. Coarse future frames model how the task may evolve over a broader temporal window.

Historical context + coarse future evolution
Local dynamics stream

How should the robot interact?

Dense frames preserve fine-grained motion around the current step, supporting alignment, grasping, and robot–object contact.

Current observation + dense short-horizon dynamics
01Encode

Each stream is encoded independently with the same pretrained video VAE.

02Coordinate

Layer-wise joint attention exchanges only the cross-stream context each query can use.

03Generate

The Action DiT predicts an action chunk conditioned on state, language, and both visual streams.

Structured cross-stream attention

Task context guides local control.
Uncertain distant futures stay indirect.

  • Long-horizon tokens attend to one another, integrating historical context and future evolution.
  • Local visual tokens access historical context and the nearest latent future frame, alongside their own stream.
  • Action tokens use both accessible visual streams, the robot state, and other action tokens. Visual tokens do not attend to actions.

Distant future latents are not directly visible to local or action tokens. This limits direct reliance on uncertain long-range predictions.

How to read the token groups

ZH,ctxt

Historical context from the long-horizon stream.

ZH,neart

Nearest coarse future latent.

ZH,fart

Distant future latents; indirect guidance only.

ZLt

Dense local visual dynamics.

qt

Proprioceptive state token.

ZAt

Action tokens for the predicted action chunk.

Training and temporal configuration +

HiLo-WAM jointly optimizes future visual prediction for both streams and action generation with flow matching. The three streams use independently sampled flow times, which supports progressive stream-wise inference.

04 / Understanding the model

Complementary information.
Measurable contributions.

Stream ablation

Future evolution brings the largest gain.

Adding coarse future evolution improves success from 90.58% to 93.22%. Historical context adds another 0.48 percentage points. Removing local dynamics from the full model reduces success by 0.18 points.

Temporal scale

A wider horizon needs enough detail.

At a fixed visual-token budget, k = 4 gives the best result among tested strides. Increasing to k = 8 makes observations too sparse and reduces performance.

Attention during execution

Task objects at one scale.
Contact regions at the other.

Action and local queries attend to task-relevant objects in the long-horizon stream. Action queries focus more on the end-effector and contact regions in the local stream.

Visual prediction

Long-horizon context gives local motion a direction.

For Place Can Basket, sparse predictions cover grasp-to-place evolution. With this context, local prediction focuses on the current grasp; without it, the predicted arm motion is less task-directed.

Progressive inference

Refine the action.
Reuse the visual context.

Visual streams stop after their assigned denoising budgets, and their representations are cached. The action stream continues to completion.

Limitations and scope +

HiLo-WAM remains limited by the semantic understanding and predictive capability of its video backbone. Tasks requiring explicit subtask decomposition, progress estimation, or higher-level reasoning remain challenging. Stronger semantic models are a natural direction for future work.

The takeaway

Look further.
Interact precisely.

Long-horizon task evolution and local dynamics carry different information. HiLo-WAM models them at different temporal scales, coordinates their interaction, and turns both into action generation.
