Abstract

Long-horizon manipulation requires both fine-grained local interaction modeling and long-range awareness of physical states and spatial relationships. World Action Models (WAMs) learn visual world dynamics by jointly modeling video and robot actions, but capturing long-range state evolution and local dynamics within a single trajectory can entangle their representations and obscure their distinct dependencies in action generation. We propose HiLo-WAM, which decouples these dynamics through multi-scale temporal modeling. A sparse long-horizon visual stream incorporates historical observations and predicts future visual state to capture how the task has progressed and where it is heading, while a dense local dynamics stream predicts short-horizon future frames to capture fine-grained interactions required for precise control. Both streams interact with actions through structured cross-stream attention to model their causal dependencies, further enabling efficient progressive inference through selective denoising and feature reuse. Experiments across multiple simulation benchmarks and long-horizon real-world tasks demonstrate HiLo-WAM’s strong performance and generalization. HiLo-WAM achieves average success rates of 93.92% on RoboTwin 2.0 and 97.6% on LIBERO, and an average progress score of 22.70 with 16.82% success across RoboDojo’s five capability dimensions. Ablations and mechanism analyses support the decoupled, structured design and highlight the importance of balancing temporal coverage and resolution for long-range state modeling.

Method

Two temporal scales, one coordinated policy

A shared Video DiT models sparse long-range states and dense local dynamics. Structured cross-stream attention coordinates both with the Action DiT.

Progressive inference stops visual refinement at stream-specific budgets and reuses cached features while completing action generation.
